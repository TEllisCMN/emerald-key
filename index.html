Emerald Key A life server where the rules were decided before the code was written.

Most life servers are assembled from a framework, a pile of mods, and whatever scripts were lying around. This one was designed. Every mechanic here exists because someone asked what it would do to the way people actually play.

The idea

Nobody knows your name until you tell them

You arrive on the island as a stranger, and you stay one. Other players see an alias, whatever you chose to be called. Your real name exists, but only people you've introduced yourself to can see it.

That single rule reshapes everything downstream. A contact list becomes something you build rather than something you're given. A disguise actually works. Police investigating a crime have to identify a suspect instead of reading a nametag.

It also means the map shows places, not people. Shops, banks, the DMV, job sites: all marked. Other players, never. If you want to know where someone is, you ask someone.

Cash is losable

What's in your pocket drops when you die and anyone can pick it up. What's in the bank is safe. Every trip out is a decision about how much to carry.

Relogging isn't death

Log out and you come back exactly as you left: same spot, same gear, same stance. Your car is still parked where you left it. Disconnecting mid-fight, though, counts as dying.

Locks are obstacles, not ownership

A locked car can be picked and hotwired. Two separate steps, so police get two chances to interrupt. Your stolen rifle still fires, and gear doesn't stop working because the wrong person is holding it.

You interact with things, not menus

Look at a door and open that door. Walk up to a car and deal with the actual handle you're standing at. One key brings up what's in front of you. No scroll-wheel hunting, no wall of options for things across the map.

The other half of that

Police have to find out who you are

If nobody knows your name, then neither do the police, and that turns a warrant into actual work. A crime gets reported against whatever the witness saw: an alias, a description, a plate.

So the department runs a real database. Officers build criminal profiles, and a profile can exist for an alias with the person behind it still unknown. "Ghost" has three robberies against them and nobody knows who Ghost is. Then someone gets identified at a traffic stop, and the file attaches to a name.

Plates get looked up against registered owners. Vehicles get flagged stolen. Officers leave notes on people. Over weeks, the department accumulates the kind of institutional memory that makes policing feel like policing.

And it cuts the other way: a mask, a change of clothes, a car that isn't yours: all of it works, because identity is something the system genuinely tracks rather than assumes.

Wanted as an alias

You're wanted as whoever they think you are. Identification is the moment the file gets a real name on it, and that moment has to be earned.

No free suspect tracking

Police get leads, not a dot on the map. A last known location, a witness, a plate that passed a camera. Finding someone is work.

Records can be tampered with

Someone senior enough can make a charge disappear. A corrupt officer isn't a bug in a system like this. It's a story that only works because the records are real.

How it's built

Four decisions that shape everything else

01

One character per person

No alt accounts, no fresh start when a reputation goes bad. What you do follows you, which is what makes a reputation worth anything.

02

You clock on to a job

A career is something you work, not something you are. Nobody on air traffic control means uncontrolled airspace. Pay follows shifts, so standing idle earns nothing.

03

Capable is permanent, authorised isn't

An off-duty officer is a civilian. Skills you earned by playing never switch off; the badge and the powers that come with it do.

04

Licences are earned, not bought

Your driving licence comes from a real driving test: follow the route, obey the limits, come back. Which makes losing it to points actually sting.

Your first ten minutes

The tutorial is the driving test

You won't be dropped in a field with a menu. New arrivals start in town, near the shops, near the job board, near other people. One instruction, not a wall of text: go and get your licence.

The test teaches you the controls, the map, and the traffic rules police actually enforce, all without pretending to be a tutorial. Driving unlicensed is a real offence, so it isn't optional busywork either.

Then you buy your first car with the money you started with, and you'll have just about enough for the cheapest one. It's the first thing that's yours, and the first thing you'd hate to lose.

No free gear

You get money, not equipment. A car you bought means something; a car you were handed doesn't.

Meet someone

Introductions are the point of the place. You start where the people are because that's the part no script can provide.

Who you can be

Three sides, and the space between them

Civilian

Mechanic, miner, trucker, realtor, fisherman, rideshare driver, or nothing at all. Refusing a career is a supported way to play, not an oversight. Some people just want to explore, deal, or run a business.

Police

Ranked and whitelisted, with the database and identification work described above. Traffic stops, warrants, impounds, speed cameras, and a rank ladder where the powers that matter sit with the people who earned them.

EMS & Fire

Independent of both. Bleeding out doesn't kill you outright. It drops you into cardiac arrest, a longer window where a medic with a defib can still save you. Firefighting is its own job, because fires happen whether or not anyone died.

The long game

Run a business. Or run the island.

The plan doesn't stop at jobs. Players will be able to register their own companies: a garage, a shop, a security firm, a haulage outfit, with employees, shared funds, and a name that means something because of how it's been run.

Manufacturing sits underneath that. Ore becomes ingots, crops become goods, and some things can only ever be crafted, never bought from a shop. A master gunsmith becomes a person other players need to find, not a menu they click. What that work is worth gets settled at an auction house, where players bid against each other rather than selling to a machine at a fixed price.

And above all of it: an elected Governor. Someone the island votes for, who sets tax rates, writes the fines and sentences police enforce, and decides how the treasury gets split between departments.

Which means the economy has politics in it. Tax too hard and people work elsewhere. Underfund the police and crime pays better. That argument is content.

Companies, not just jobs

A career is what you do. A company is something you build, staff, and can lose. Shared funds mean trusting people, which means betrayal is possible, which is the point.

Craft-only goods

If the shop sells it, crafting it is pointless. So the best gear, tuning parts and specialist tools exist only through someone's workshop.

Bid as your alias

The auction house is anonymous by default. Nobody needs to know who just paid a fortune for that rifle, or who sold it.

A treasury worth robbing

Tax revenue is real money sitting somewhere. It follows that someone will eventually try to take it.

We're hiring

Some of these roles are open right now

A server this size doesn't run on one person, and the people who join early end up shaping how their department works rather than inheriting someone else's rules. If you've done any of this before, or just want to, come and say so in the Discord.

Police command

Senior officers to build the department: ranks, training, what a good stop looks like, and how internal discipline works. You'd be writing the policy, not just following it.

EMS & Fire leadership

Someone to run the medical side: response standards, training new medics, and shaping the fire service from scratch as it gets built.

Staff & moderation

Admins who can handle a report fairly and calmly. The tooling exists (logs, reports, a proper queue) so the job is judgement, not detective work.

Builders & developers

Eden mapping, compositions, and interiors for the island. If you write SQF, the framework is open on GitHub and there's plenty left to build.

Also worth a message if you want to run something in-world rather than behind the scenes: a gang, a business, a garage, a news outfit. Early players get to establish what those are before anyone else does.

If you like the idea that rules should make sense

Emerald Key is for people who want the world to hold together: where robbing a bank is hard because banks are hard, where police work is work, and where the person you met last week remembers you.

It's being built carefully rather than quickly. If that sounds like your kind of server, come and help shape what it becomes.
